History-Conditioned World Model for Adaptive Quadruped Locomotion

This project investigates how a quadruped can use limited data from physical interactions to adapt its control during terrain transitions. Our model-based reinforcement learning approach plans in the latent space of a world model conditioned on a transformer encoding of recent actions and state changes. In simulation, the history-conditioned model predicted motion more accurately than the fixed model under shifts in friction and actuator strength, including actuator strengths outside the training range. For our quadruped locomotion through mud project with Field AI, we use Newton to evaluate transitions from firm ground into mud and between patches with different resistance to foot withdrawal. We are investigating whether conditioning the world model on another leg’s contact history at a planned foothold improves predictions for the next step.

Closed-Loop-Compliant Reinforcement Learning

A robot holding a tool may need to yield at its elbow when pushed while keeping its hand on target. I post-train an existing policy using paired simulations that start from the same state, with an external force applied to one robot. The difference between their task-space motions isolates the response to the force. I train a residual policy over the frozen base policy to match that response to a virtual mass–spring–damper model, which specifies how the robot should yield and recover after release. I use the resulting method to study how robots can accommodate contact while retaining their original skills.
