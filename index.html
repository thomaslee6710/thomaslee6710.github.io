I want to build robots that can take the skills they’ve learned into places they’ve never worked before.

In the Robomechanics Lab, advised by Prof. Aaron Johnson, I study how robots can use a few physical interactions to decide what to do next. I work on learned world models and control for robots moving through unfamiliar terrain and responding to contact.

Choose an interaction

A foot sinks on contact. That observation can help a world model predict how deeply the next foot might sink under a different load.

From physical experience to action

A robot can practice a familiar task thousands of times and still encounter ground that yields differently or an object that resists its grasp. I’m interested in what a world model should remember from that first contact. If a controller can predict how the world will respond to several possible actions, it can use that brief experience to choose its next move.
