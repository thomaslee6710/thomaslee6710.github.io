Thomas Chun Fai Lee

I’m an M.S. student in Robotics at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Aaron Johnson. I study how robots can use brief physical interactions to decide how to act in unfamiliar environments. A foot sinking into mud or a leg catching on a vine can change what the robot should do next.

Predicting the next step on changing ground

When a quadruped steps from firm ground into mud, the same joint command can produce a different motion.

I use a transformer to summarize recent commands and observed motion. A latent dynamics model uses that history to predict how the robot will move under candidate commands, and a model predictive controller uses those predictions to choose its next action.

Research in physical interaction

Learning to yield under contact

I developed a post-training method that gives an existing policy a tunable response to an external force. I compare paired rollouts with and without the force to isolate the motion it causes, then train a residual policy to match that motion to a target mass–spring–damper response while the robot performs its task.

Recovering a caught leg

I led hardware evaluation of a quadruped policy that learns to escape entanglements. I compared contact estimates decoded from each leg’s latent representation with video of the moment a cord caught and released it, then tested recovery in natural vegetation.

Balancing on a narrow beam

I designed gait policies and a selector for duty factor, the fraction of each stride a foot spends on the ground. Across 45 hardware trials on a 15 cm beam, we studied how contact duration affected crossing success using centroidal MPC without dedicated balance actuation.
