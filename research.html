Conditioning World Model for Adaptive Quadruped Locomotion Across Changing Terrains

This project investigates how a quadruped can use limited data from physical interactions to adapt its control during terrain transitions. Our model-based reinforcement learning approach plans in the latent space of a world model conditioned on a transformer encoding of recent actions and state changes.

In simulation, history conditioning improved motion prediction over the fixed model under changes in friction and actuator strength, including actuator strengths outside the training range. After mid-episode changes, prediction error fell by 11–21% for the tested reductions in actuator strength and by 43% for the tested drop in friction. With Field AI, we use Newton to evaluate quadruped locomotion across firm-ground-to-mud transitions and mud patches with varying foot withdrawal resistance.

We are investigating two ways to condition the world model for adaptive control. The first uses reinforcement learning to select which recent state transitions to retain after a terrain change. The second retrieves contact history from another leg that previously contacted the planned foothold to improve predictions for the next step.

Closed-Loop-Compliant Reinforcement Learning

This project investigates how to give an existing robot policy a tunable response to external forces while preserving its nominal behavior. We compare paired rollouts from the same initial state to isolate force-induced task-space motion. We train a residual over the frozen policy so that this motion tracks a virtual mass–spring–damper response, with fixed low-level gains.

In Isaac Lab, a 30 N trunk push displaced the post-trained Go2 by 14.0 cm against a 14.8 cm reference, compared with 2.1 cm for the base policy. On a Franka reaching task, post-training reduced compliance tracking error under a 15 N hand push from 6.1 cm to 1.06 cm, with nominal tracking error near 1 cm. We also demonstrate elbow yielding while maintaining the hand target and G1 locomotion under 50 N palm pulls.

We are investigating how to specify compliant responses that respect task constraints and whether the same post-training objective can shape skills learned by vision-language-action policies.
